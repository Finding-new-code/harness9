The Vacuum Tube Bottleneck

The Vacuum Tube Bottleneck. Before the computer in your pocket, early computers filled entire rooms with burning hot, glass vacuum tubes. Early vacuum tubes consumed large amounts of power and generated high heat; transistors slashed power consumption by over 99% and miniaturized circuits.

PART 02 • HARNESS 9

The Miracle at Bell Labs

The Miracle at Bell Labs. On two cold days in December 1947, three physicists made history with a sliver of germanium, gold foil, and a paper clip. The first working point-contact transistor was successfully demonstrated on December 23, 1947, by John Bardeen and Walter Brattain at Bell Laboratories.

100 Billion Strong

100 Billion Strong. Today, that single rough prototype has scaled to over 100 billion microscopic switches powering every AI and phone on Earth. By 2026, leading-edge semiconductor chips pack over 100 billion transistors onto a silicon die smaller than a postage stamp.
